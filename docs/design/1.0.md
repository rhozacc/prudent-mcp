# prudent-mcp 1.0: playbook-first, eight tools, domain language

**Status:** proposed (2026-10-05). It is the server half of a suite-wide 1.0. The full plan, evidence and the other repos' work packages live in the private factory repo. This page is self-contained for work in this repo, and it is **corpus-agnostic by design**: no record counts, no corpus ids, no corpus text. The licence (AGPL-3.0-only), the read-only boundary and strict TypeScript are unchanged.

## Why

The server answers lookups well and synthesis questions poorly. Measured on a real corpus with a practitioner's question whose answer spans four instruments:

1. **The client model does the synthesis at query time, under a context budget.** It retrieved part of the relevant material, stopped, and answered from that part. The section that answered half the question was never retrieved.
2. **Search is structure-blind.**
   - Paragraphs inherit their meaning from a sub-section heading that records do not carry, so a paragraph that never repeats the subject's word cannot be found.
   - Ranking (coverage first, then a raw score sum: no IDF, no length normalisation) lets long, multi-topic, non-normative records (background, consultation feedback) outrank short operative paragraphs.
3. **Playbooks are pointer bags.** They are per-document phase groupings of reference ids. Reading one costs dozens of fetches, and none crosses documents.
4. **The server's own vocabulary leaks into answers.**
   - The instructions ask the model to label statements "not corpus-backed".
   - Notes say "not ingested here".
   - A document-level pending-change note rides on provisions the change does not touch.

   Answers then talk about the system instead of the regulation.
5. **The evals measure truthfulness at the edges, not completeness in the middle.** Every invariant is green while a realistic question misses most of what it needs.

The fix moves synthesis to build time. The factory compiles a **playbook** per topic: cross-document, self-contained, every statement cited, verified deterministically, approved by the corpus owner. The server leads with it. One test of this on one question: the same model, handed one compiled playbook (about 6k tokens) instead of the 0.10 tool loop, went from a partial, leaky answer to a complete one with no plumbing language, graded blind by two judges. The factory repo holds the experiment.

## What 1.0 changes

### Surface: 19 tools → 8

| 1.0 | replaces | contract |
|---|---|---|
| `brief(question, as_of?, budget?)` | — | First call for any substantive question. Returns the playbook that answers it (rendered text), the provisions that matter but are not in it, and notes |
| `playbook(id, section?)` | `get_playbook`, `expand_playbook`, `get_area_overview` | The whole playbook rendered, or one requirement with its provisions' full text |
| `topics()` | `list_review_areas` | Areas → playbooks: id, title, one-line scope |
| `search(query, scope?, document?, limit?, offset?)` | the four `search_*` | Ranked passages. The default scope is provisions, with background excluded. The 0.x envelope stays (`total_matches`, `next_offset`, `best_coverage`) |
| `get(ids, as_of?)` | `get_regulation`, `get_check`, `get_test`, `get_source` | Up to 20 records of any surface, size-capped |
| `cite(text)` | `resolve_citation` | The same resolver, renamed. Its gates and the I3 suite are unchanged |
| `related(id)` | `get_referrers`, `expand_regulation`, `get_regulation_tree` | Heading path and siblings, cross-references both ways, primary checks/tests, playbooks citing it, amendments affecting it |
| `sources(id?)` | `get_corpus_info`, `list_sources`, `get_source` | What the library holds per document (whole, part or undeclared), versions, what is changing and when |

- `get_coverage_gaps` leaves the server; it is an operations metric and moves to the console.
- The three validator-shaped prompts become one, `walk_through(topic)`.
- Resource templates stay.
- The standing surface target is ≤ 2,500 tokens (0.10: about 6,150).

### Instructions: about 1,300 tokens → ≤ 350

> Prudent: EU regulation and supervisory practice for internal ratings-based (IRB) credit risk — the CRR, EBA guidelines and the ECB guide to internal models — with a playbook per topic.
> For any substantive question, call `brief` first: it returns the playbook that answers it and the provisions that matter. Expand with `playbook`; open specific provisions with `search`, `get` or `cite`; use `related` for neighbouring provisions and `sources` for what the library covers and what is changing.
> When you answer: cite provisions by their official citation (e.g. "EBA/GL/2017/16 para 28", "Art. 179(1)(d) CRR"), never by internal identifiers, and do not describe the tools. Keep law, EBA guidelines, ECB supervisory expectations and market practice distinct, as the playbook labels them. When a note says a provision is amended or about to be, say so with the date and what changes. When the question turns on an instrument the library does not hold, name it and answer from general knowledge, marked as such.

The edge-of-corpus rules in 0.10's instructions (scope, holdings, as_of, placeholders, force vs register) do not disappear. They move into the **notes** each response carries, at the point they apply, and they are written so they can be repeated to a user verbatim.

### Notes: one field, written for the end user

`notes: { type: "amendment" | "version" | "outside_library" | "placeholder" | "weak_match", text, applies_to? }[]` replaces `as_of_note`, `pending_changes_note`, `pre_adoption_placeholders` + `notice`, `coverage_note` and the search notice sentences.

**Language rule.** Server-authored text never contains: *corpus, ingest/ingested, registry, record(s), holdings, adapter, served, this server*, or tool names. It names instruments, provisions and dates. "This library" is the only self-reference.

**Amendment notes are provision-level.** They are computed from `amends` on the amending instrument's records (schema below). A provision no amendment targets carries no amendment note. A change the source entry knows about without a provision mapping is stated once, in `sources`, and in `brief` when relevant, naming the paragraphs from the factory-parsed `affects_ids`. The server still never parses free text.

### Schema (corpus format 2, read alongside format 1 during transition)

- **`Regulation`** gains three optional fields:
  - `heading_path: string[]`: verbatim outline headings;
  - `role: "operative" | "definition" | "scope" | "transitional" | "background" | "annex"`;
  - `amends?: { target, op: "replace" | "insert_after" | "delete", effective_from }[]`.
- **`Source`** gains optional `citation_style` (how ids render as official citations; for example, a guide's chapter names) and `PendingChange.affects_ids`.
- **`Playbook`** is redefined (breaking): `id: playbook://{topic}`, plus `title`, `area`, `summary`, `questions`, `applies_to`, `basis[{citation, force, role, provisions[{id, quote?}]}]`, `requirements[{id: R\d+, title, statement, provisions, evidence, checks, tests}]`, `methods[{name, description, basis: regulatory|practice, provisions}]`, `pitfalls`, `related`, `outside_library`, `excluded`, `provenance{status, compiled_at, compiler, inputs_sha, approved_*}`. The full zod is in M2.1.
- **The corpus file** gains `topics` (areas → topic ids, titles, scope) and `glossary` (abbreviation → phrases).
- **`Phase`, `ReviewArea` and the per-document playbook shape are removed.** `scripts/schema-registry.ts` and `docs/schemas/` are regenerated (`bun run schemas`).

### Search: BM25F, still one module

`rankedSearch` becomes BM25F in `src/search.ts`:
- **Fields:** `heading_path` weight 3, citation 2, text 1, commentary 0.3.
- **Scoring:** IDF; per-field length normalisation with b = 0.75 and k1 = 1.2; glossary OR-expansion; light stemming applied identically at index and query time.
- **Priors:** `role: background` is excluded unless `scope: "all"`; `kind: section` × 0.35; `is_metadata_only` × 0.5.
- **Still true:** ranking is uncapped, the tool layer pages, `coverage` and `best_coverage` remain in the envelope as signals, and `makeExcerpt` is unchanged.

### `brief`

1. **Route.** Combine BM25F over each playbook's title, questions, summary and requirement text with a membership vote: the provision search hits that are members of each playbook, weighted 1/rank.
2. **Select.** Take the top playbook when it clears a floor and beats the runner-up by a margin. Both constants are tuned on a practitioner question bank, and the bank version is recorded in code.
3. **Render** within a budget (2.5k / 5k / 9k tokens) by a deterministic renderer:
   - citations are rendered from ids through `citation_style`, never written by a model;
   - the body carries no internal ids;
   - requirements collapse to title plus one line when over budget, with an expansion handle (`playbook(id, "R7")`).
4. **Append** the top 3–5 provisions the playbook does not cite, up to two neighbouring playbooks, and notes.
5. **If no playbook clears the floor,** say so plainly and return the top passages grouped by document.

`structuredContent` carries ids for programmatic clients.

**Kept, remapped to new tool names:**
- I1, I3 (all sub-checks, on `cite`), I4, I5, I7;
- I11 (version notes);
- I12 (partly-held documents: the decline names the instrument and says it is held in part, in domain words);
- I13 (`weak_match`), I14 (`placeholder`);
- I15 (amendment notes provision-level: present on targets, absent on non-targets, absent under an `as_of` before the change);
- I16.

**I6** is re-based: the surface is ≤ 2,500 tokens, plus a per-call budget for `brief`.

**I8** stays as the "own words" floor. The practitioner-phrasing measurement (context recall against expert provision maps) lives in the benchmarking repo and runs on the real corpus. It is not public, because its maps name corpus content.

**New:**
- **I17, language.** No banned term in any server-authored string: instructions, descriptions, notes, notices, miss and decline texts. Record text is excluded.
- **I18, playbook integrity.** Every served playbook passes `verifyPlaybook` against the served corpus.
- **I19, self-routing.** For every served playbook and each of its `questions`, `brief(question)` selects it.

**The demo corpus** (`examples/inmemory-demo.ts`) gains one topic, one playbook, a glossary, `heading_path` and `role` on its records, and one amending record, so I15/I17–I19 bind in CI.
